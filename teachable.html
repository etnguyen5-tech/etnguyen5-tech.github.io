Teachable Machine: Hand Gesture Recognizer

We've recently noticed that Zoom and other live video apps have been rolling out gesture-based reactions to use in tandem with the reaction buttons themselves. Use cases for gestures are wide ranging, from ASL, to VR/MR interactions with augmented interfaces, to human-robot interaction, and even everyday integrations in smart home and car systems, like spinning your finger to adjust the volume.

To better understand how gestures are interpreted by AI, we decided to train an image classifier using Google's Teachable Machine, as demoed by The Coding Train.

Project Scope

  • The scope of our project includes four common hand gestures: thumbs up, thumbs down, peace sign, and open palm (raised hand). We also added a "no hand" class, since the model always has to pick one of its classes, so without it, it would guess a gesture even when no one is showing one.
    Each of us took 100 photos of ourselves on our own webcams (20 per class), so our dataset is 300 original photos total. To try it out, click allow camera access and show one of the gestures!
